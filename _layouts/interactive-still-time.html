There's Still Ti:me

I've been reading about clocks. Chirstopher Butler wrote about his Fifteen Clocks which got me thinking about non-12 hour clocks and what else they could show. I subscribe to the Rest Is Science podcast and there's an issue where Michael Stevens talks about his unusual clocks. I also read Craftland which had a chapter on Roger W. Smith. Who makes handmade watches from scratch. Not from components from raw metal. His factory produces just fifteen to twenty watches every year. Some of the watches take years to make.

All this was coincidental, but I'm at the point of seeing there's a lot more behind me than there's likely to be in front of me. I like the idea of showing the passage of time in different periods. This is my place to play with clocks that show different aspects of time.

The quality/price of a watch is often seen as how smoothly the second hand rotates. It used to be how you could distinguish a quartz from a mechanical mechanism. This is no longer case. For a long time the only place I would see a smooth second hand was the pace clock at a public swimming pool. They're still incredibly soothing. Not like the incessenct tick-tick of an exam clock.

Is it always darkest just before dawn?

At the crack of dawn, a false dawn, dawn on someone, the twilight of your life. There's so much meaning given to dusk and dawn. They still feel special even if we get two of them each day.

Is it tea time yet?

Meal times are an artificial marker of time, a human made dusk and dawn.
