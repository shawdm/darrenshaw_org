## Welcome to the AI Bitch

The more I do with AI, the less special I think the human brain is. I've read arguments that as clever as we
think we are, we're all
just [prediction machines](https://www.psy.ox.ac.uk/news/the-brain-is-a-prediction-machine-it-knows-how-good-we-are-doing-something-before-we-even-try)
too. I've read arguments
that [consciousness is just a trick to help us process that prediction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphenomenalism).
AI can never be creative? I asked ChatGPT, "What would Summer Roberts say if Blair Waldorf got in her
way? Give me two options."

1. "Look, headband, I don't know who you think you are in your little Upper East Side bubble, but I'm from Newport Beach
   and you are literally blocking my path to Barneys."
2. "I have zero time for your weird power trips. Go find your boyfriend with the scarf and bother him."

"Headband". Amazing.

## Reading

1. [Regime Change: Inside the Imperial Presidency of Donald Trump](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/250673180-regime-change).
   Everything you already know. Everything you already imagine. All in one place.
2. [1996: My Backstage Pass to The Wildest Year of Britain’s Wildest Decade](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/243511792-1996).
   This is pretty bland. It's 90s nostalgia you already know about with little criticism. I did like all the "10 best"
   lists.
3. [Harvest](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/241082567-harvest). Minette Batters was the first female president of
   the NFU. I could have skipped the background/life story part, but the rest offered insight into how farmers think. I
   wish farmers with experience outside of farming were more vocal.
4. [Broken Country](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/214151202). I don't think I liked the main character in the way
   you're meant to. But I didn't see the twist coming. Worth reading.
5. [The Names](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/217245618-the-names) is another spin on the replaying your life
   theme I love. Dark, but a page turner.
6. [The agents are coming for the web and the web isn’t ready](https://redmonk.com/jgovernor/the-agents-are-coming-for-the-web-and-the-web-isnt-ready/).
   This is a good summary of a problem that exists today and is getting worse rapidly. It may need reframing as a
   problem to be managed as we move to a new normal. The valuable reading is in the links.
7. Also from
   RedMonk, [Agents are choosing the software infrastructure now, and we’re not really ready](https://redmonk.com/jgovernor/agents-are-choosing-the-software-infrastructure-now-and-were-not-really-ready/).
   Just interesting. Totally different thinking.
8. [Do you even need a presentation?](https://martinfowler.com/articles/never-send-slides/need-presentation.html).
   I don't see presentations sent as documents often now. Is that the culture in a different company or have
   things changed? In the consultancy part of IBM in the 2010s it was the primary means of communication.
9. [Agent Skills](https://addyosmani.com/blog/agent-skills/) is my monthly recommendation from Addy Osmani. It's an AI
   skills post, but it was a reminder of some of Google's engineering practices: the Beyoncé Rule, DAMP over DRY in
   tests, ~100-line PR sizing, Code-as-liability.
10. Work-relevant
    reading: [Balancing Multiple Objectives in Generative Recommendations with Adaptive Decoding](https://research.atspotify.com/2026/8/balancing-multiple-objectives-in-generative-recommendations-with-adaptive-decoding)
