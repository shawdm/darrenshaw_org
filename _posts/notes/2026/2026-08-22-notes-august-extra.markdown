## Reading

1. [One Golden Summer](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/217245639-one-golden-summer) by Carley Fortune. Prime
   beach read material. I read [Every Summer After](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58014893-every-summer-after) a
   couple of years ago and don't remember anything about it other than it wasn't as good
   as [The Summer I Turned Pretty](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/5821978-the-summer-i-turned-pretty). One
   Golden Summer was so generic I kept trying to work out if I've somehow read it before. I'm really just looking for
   another Dawson's Creek, The OC or Gossip Girl.
2. [The Swedish Art of Ageing Well](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/62929392-the-swedish-art-of-ageing-well). This
   is an autobiography by a normal person who has obvious but sensible advice. I don't know how it ever got published
   but it was charming and refreshing in its mundaneness.
3. [Why play is the core of a meaningful life](https://www.ft.com/content/7b95cdab-7546-432f-b4a1-6509e7648ec5). It's such a
   good read. The point of life is to play and it's what still makes humans different to AI. The FT Weekend is £5.40.
   It's the best money I spend every week (see two more references below).
4. [Nested Vectors Search In Apache Lucene](https://sease.io/2026/05/nested-vectors-search-in-apache-lucene.html). Work
   related. Possible solution to handling multiple versions of embeddings we've been thinking about.
5. [Don't Outsource the Learning](https://addyosmani.com/blog/dont-outsource-learning/). There are so many Addy Osmani
   articles on AI I link to. I like the closing line of this one, _I've started ending coding sessions with a simple
   question: did I learn anything today, or did I just close the issue?_
6. [Shaping Product Understanding with Contrastive Reinforcement Learning](https://www.etsy.com/codeascraft/shaping-product-understanding-with-contrastive-reinforcement-learning).
   Etsy have some good nerd content. Another (maybe) work-relevant one.
7. [‘Have I been influenced, or is this actually me?’ How personal taste fell out of fashion](https://www.theguardian.com/media/2026/jun/14/have-i-been-influenced-personal-taste-out-of-fashion-algorithm).
   This Guardian article made me think of the people I worked with at Net-A-Porter who really knew fashion and
   curation. _If you dismiss taste as trivial, or see it as purely imitative, you are dismissing what it is to be a
   person._ Could this be related to Eugene
   Rabkin's [Is the luxury world stuck on repeat?](https://www.ft.com/content/cf99c741-eae5-4abb-a8a8-08c65157b705)
   (also in the FT Weekend)? If nobody curates their own style, how could fashion not be stuck?
8. The same issue of the FT Weekend had an article about [On-Togther](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zg31vpFaxwM), a
   virtual co-working game/not-game. Which sounded a lot cuter and less nerdy than Second Life.
9. [Dancing with friends and enemies](https://community.wolfram.com/groups/-/m/t/122095). Swarming algorithms seem to be
   getting attention like it's 2001 all over again.
