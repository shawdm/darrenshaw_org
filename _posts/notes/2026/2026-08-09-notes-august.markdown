## Watching

1. [The Drama](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt33071426/). I'll watch anything with Zendaya. This film has a great setup.

   Zendaya's character. I imagine people will feel differently, but I was on her side.
2. [Euphoria Season 3](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt8772296/episodes/?season=3). Have you heard I'll watch anything with
   Zendaya in it? Season 3 faced a lot
   of [criticism](https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2026/apr/13/euphoria-season-three-review-sydney-sweeney-zendaya-hbo)

   shocking, as the first two. But I love how beautifully made it is. Even films don't look like films in 2026, but this
   is a TV series that still looks like a film.

   This scene is brilliant. The colours are great, it's styled perfectly, each cut uses the other characters as frames
   and there's just so many layers and interactions going on. Look at how Rue, Maddy and Lexi crouch (or sit) down so
   that the frame works with Cassie and the leaf blower girl in the background. I love the bit where Maddy doesn't say
   anything but gives Rue, Cassie and leaf blower different individual looks and then makes the start your engine

## Reading

I was on holiday this month and so I read quite a bit.

1. [Yesteryear](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/238226942-yesteryear) by Caro Claire Burke. This has got a lot of
   criticism but it was fun. Think of Inception but about social media tradwives. There'll be a film.
2. [Down to Earth](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/34879206-down-to-earth) by Monty Don. Catching up on 40 years of
   missing gardening knowledge with books. What I'll take from Monty Don is mainly, mulch.
3. [How to Art](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/223447922-how-to-art) by Kate Bryan. There are few books I find
   genuinely inspiring. This has been one of them. I'm now forever browsing [Avant Arte](https://avantarte.com)
   and [JEALOUS](https://jealousgallery.com).
4. [Craftland](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/222955929-craftland) by James Fox. I thought I wasn't going to
   enjoy this after the first couple of pages. Glad I kept going, I loved it.
5. The summer that changed me was in the [FT's Life & Arts](https://www.ft.com/life-arts) weekend
   supplement. [Listicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listicle) got a bad name in the 2010s for being an easy way to
   write clickbaity posts, a quaint concern looking back from 2026. This is a listicle done well. FT's
   journalists write a couple of paragraphs about a significant summer memory. Insightful nonsense that I love.
6. [The Factory Model: How Coding Agents Changed Software Engineering](https://addyosmani.com/blog/factory-model/) is
   similar to a lot of stuff written in the last year about software development and AI. But I enjoyed the _new shape of
   high-leverage engineering_ section which lists the skills that continue to set the best engineers apart:
   systems thinking, problem decomposition, architectural judgement, specification clarity, output evaluation and
   orchestration skill. I'd add in product/problem understanding.
7. [Japanese symbols that speak without words](https://arun.is/blog/japan-symbols/) introduced me to
   the [Ba no kuuki wo yomu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba_no_kuuki_wo_yomu) which translates to understanding the
   situation without words. It's where something happens and everyone just knows the correct way to respond.
8. [I Could've Rickrolled the Entire FIFA World Cup](https://bobdahacker.com/blog/fifa-hack) because FIFA haven't had
   enough drama this month.
9. [Boosted K-Nearest Neighbour Search](https://sease.io/2026/07/boosted-k-nearest-neighbor-search.html) described
   exactly the problem we're trying to tackle at work but then said there's not really an answer.
10. [Protect Your Shed](https://dylanbutler.dev/blog/protect-your-shed/). When I've worked in great development teams the
   engineers have always built their own sheds.
11. [The Economics of Software Teams](https://www.viktorcessan.com/the-economics-of-software-teams/) is a clear-sighted
   way of looking at software development, but I don't think it captures the cost of day-to-day firefighting for a team running
   their software in the real world.
